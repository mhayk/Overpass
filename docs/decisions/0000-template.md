# NNNN — Short title, phrased as the decision

- **Status:** proposed | accepted | rejected | deprecated | superseded by [ADR-NNNN](NNNN-title.md)
- **Date:** YYYY-MM-DD
- **Deciders:** who actually made the call
- **Supersedes:** ADR-NNNN, if this reverses an earlier decision

## Context and problem statement

What forced a decision here. One or two paragraphs, written so that someone
who joins the project in six months understands the pressure that existed at
the time — not the answer, the pressure.

State the question explicitly, as a question.

## Decision drivers

- The constraints and qualities that actually mattered, in rough priority order
- Include the uncomfortable ones (time budget, team familiarity, my own fluency)
- If a driver is a guess rather than a measurement, say so here

## Considered options

1. **Option A** — one line
2. **Option B** — one line
3. **Option C** — one line

## Decision outcome

Chosen: **Option X**, because ...

One paragraph. The reason must be traceable to a decision driver above. If it
is not, either the driver list or the reason is wrong.

### Consequences

**Good**

- What this buys us, concretely

**Bad**

- What this costs us, concretely. An ADR with no costs listed is marketing,
  not engineering.

**Neutral**

- Things that change but are neither wins nor losses

### Confirmation

How we will know this decision was right or wrong — a test, a benchmark, a
metric, or an explicit trigger that would make us revisit it. If nothing could
falsify the decision, it was not a decision, it was a preference.
